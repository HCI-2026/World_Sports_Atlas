About this project

A group project for our Human–Computer Interaction course, on the theme of Internationalization.

Why traditional sports?

Sports travel. Muay Thai gyms now open in Europe, Brazilian jiu-jitsu grew out of Japanese judo, and Basque pelota became jai alai in Florida. Each of these games carries rituals and values from the place it began, and they often keep them when they move abroad.

This site collects 33 traditional sports and martial arts from six continents. For each one we explain what it is and how you win, then cover its rituals, the values behind it, how it spread to other countries, and where you can watch it today.

How to use the map

  1. On the home page, click a continent or one of the continent buttons above the map.
  2. The map zooms in and gives each country that a sport comes from its own colour. The number on each country shows how many sports it has.
  3. Hover over a number or a coloured country to see the list of sports.
  4. Hover a sport to preview it in the side panel, and click it to keep the panel open. A panel shows a photo, the basics of how the sport is played, facts, key terms, a video link and the sources.

Prefer a list? Every sport is also listed under All sports.

Where the information comes from

Facts are taken from English Wikipedia, and every sport links to the page it came from. Where Wikipedia does not explain the rules of a sport, we used another rules page and list it as well. Photos are loaded from each sport's Wikipedia article and link to their Wikimedia Commons file page. The full list is on the Sources page.
